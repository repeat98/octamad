## DSP budget and architecture, from bamshanks directly (17 Sep 2026, ~13:48–14:39)

Firsthand, from the project maintainer, describing his own in-progress re-architecture (not yet flashed/finalized — he says "hopefully finished last night" and still pressure-testing):

- **DSP cycle budget: "3120 cycles per core"** — a DSP budget "separate to the main coldfire processor." If divided evenly across inserts, "that would mean 390 per insert," but "some are very cheap so you can spread it round."
- **Word budget**: plentynights recalled from the repo docs "3k for FX1 and 16k for FX2" words, which bamshanks confirmed ("yeah something like that... there is a bunch of shared though"). Treat as an approximate figure sourced from the project's own docs, not a new measurement.
- **Modulation and parameter locks are handled by the ColdFire, not the DSP** — bamshanks, direct answer to a direct question ("What about modulation and parameter locks? Are they also handled by the DSP?" → "coldfire").
- **The stock delay is an exception to the DSP-effects model**: "it runs on the coldfire and has it's own block of ram like the recorders... not my delay but the stock one." This says the *stock* Octatrack delay effect is implemented on the ColdFire (not the DSP) with a dedicated RAM block analogous to the recorder buffers — a hardware/firmware architecture claim, not previously seen phrased this way in the visible CLAUDE.md traps. **May relate to existing trap docs — cross-check** against `docs/firmware/` DSP/delay architecture notes to see whether this ColdFire-hosted-stock-delay fact is already recorded.
- **His new effect re-architecture** (screenshots posted, not OCR'd from this export): FX1 slot per track hosts an "insert" he calls a "station" (his term for a multi-effect, as opposed to a single stock-like effect). On the DSP side, core 0's FX2 hosts a delay and core 1's FX2 hosts a reverb, in a "hard coded bus setup." Every FX2 track has a "send" knob feeding that bus. The master channel has a "character" effect (compressor + wavefolding + drive) with its own "return" knob feeding back into the bus. He notes character is "expensive" and he "budgeted basically for character on every track, as that's the worst of [his] inserts," following "the elektron way of leaving some headroom under the worst conditions."
- **Free word budget is tight and shrinking fast**: bryant12345 (16 Sep, 20:29–21:07): "I think there are under 500 free words on Payload A... it took all of one day to get to 400." (This is a rough recollection, not a precise measurement — flagged as such by his own phrasing "I think.")
- **Local emulation vs hardware timing still diverges**: bamshanks (17 Sep, 14:29–14:30), on cycle-budget confidence: "I'm not at 100% confidence on the numbers yet... What happens on my emulator is not exactly matched on the hardware. Every time I think I map it, something does something a lil different and the notes get bigger." This is a general, first-hand caveat about the reliability of emulator-measured cycle counts vs hardware reality — relevant to the project's own "believe the hardware" instrumentation-blindness principle.
- **Neither bamshanks's nor plentynights's independent emulator runs in real time as of 17 Sep 2026** (both tested and reported "not realtime lol" within minutes of each other, comparing notes).

## Part-change / REC SETUP bug (Sezare56 / bukkakeborealis, 14 Sep 2026)

Firsthand, reported by Sezare56 as an OKMS (octakit + midi scenes remix) user-facing bug, being chased by zackyoti_98392:

- "Buggy behaviors with REC SETUP and part changes. The last tweaked value (RLEN for instance) can be maintained after a part change, without updating the new part value." Example given: Pattern 1/Part 1 SRC=T1, Pattern 2/Part 2 SRC=T2; tweak pattern 1's SRC to T8, then switch to pattern 2 — pattern 2 records T8 instead of its own stored T2. "That's why it can work after a power cycle. Maybe SAVE/RELOAD parts, bank or project works too."
- Clarified further (21:13): "rec setup displayed values are updated with part change, but previous part 'tweaked' settings are applied" — i.e., the UI redraws the new part's stored value, but the *DSP/ColdFire-side runtime state* still holds the old part's last-tweaked value until something (a power cycle, or presumably a trig/reload) resets it.
- bukkakeborealis (21:24): "tried at some point getting part change to take effect on key press but... they do need a trig even if screen shows the new values instantly" — i.e., his own related finding that some track-setting changes only take effect on a trig, consistent with the general "value published but not effective until X" family of surprises already documented in CLAUDE.md (e.g., "a slot can draw a knob and publish nothing" and "sequencer stalls" part-layout traps). **May relate to existing trap docs — cross-check** against the CLAUDE.md entry "A PART SAVED UNDER AN OLDER SLOT LAYOUT..." and "THE PART THE EMULATED LOAD APPLIES IS NOT THE PART THAT PLAYS" — this is a different bug (REC SETUP specifically, not slot layout or bank/part loading) but same general class of stale-runtime-state-across-part-change behavior. This is octakit/midi-scenes-side, not octabam DSP, but the underlying part/pattern state-refresh mechanism it exposes may be shared firmware behavior worth noting if octabam ever touches REC SETUP or part-change handling.

## Hardware architecture claims from plentynights's independent emulator/RE effort (17 Sep 2026)

New contributor `plentynights` is running a separate, from-scratch reverse-engineering effort on the Octatrack (Ghidra decompile of 1.4C firmware, a custom emulator, and a semi-working class-compliant USB Audio CFW). His claims are firsthand from his own RE work but **have not been cross-verified by bamshanks/octabam** — worth treating as independent testimony pending confirmation:

- **Display is not directly wired to the ColdFire MCU; it's driven via SPI through a companion chip.** "Graphics are pretty complex actually on octa as I assumed the display was directly connected to the MCU... but it's via SPI on the companion." Cost him significant time ("It took me way too much time to get a working display out").
- He extracted the full boot animation frame-by-frame from the firmware and believes he's "the first to extract the entire boot animation" (unverified claim, but plausible given screenshots posted of boot sequence, splash bitmap, and named UI assets like `asset1_54x25.png`..`asset5_52x22.png`).
- **USB audio CFW status** (semi-working, "not working reliably enough yet"): currently class-compliant, 2-channel only, "on track 8 using the track 8 master mode." Confirmed UAC2.0 and High-Speed USB. He states "multitrack is technically possible since we're doing UAC2.0 and HS USB... it's just a matter of getting data efficiently out" (his own assessment, not yet demonstrated).
- **USB audio tap point**: "right now it's pre-fx track 8 and haven't been able to find a better place... I believe via recording arming u get additional taps but haven't checked yet the exact mechanism... ideally we get a tap post-fx for the main out." bamshanks noted this would make the OT "the first elektron to get post fx" USB audio if achieved — neither party was certain whether Overbridge on other Elektron boxes is pre- or post-FX (bamshanks: "don't think so, but I never really used ob when I had digis... but from mem it was all pre and the fx on a separate track, coz bus" — explicitly speculative/from memory).
- He shared a document `TIMESTRETCH_PIPELINE.md` (16 Sep, 16:40) as a Discord attachment describing (his words) the OT's timestretch algorithm mapping, generated via LLM analysis of the firmware, admittedly imperfect ("some of it may be not exactly right especially for exact values / roles but I've found it to be fairly reliable... sometimes doesn't name things right"). **Note: this exact filename (`docs/TIMESTRETCH_PIPELINE.md`) already exists as an untracked file in the octamad working tree per git status** — meaning this attachment has apparently already been pulled into the project. Given plentynights's own caveat about reliability, if this file's content came directly from that attachment, it should be treated as an unverified/LLM-derived hypothesis about the timestretch pipeline until confirmed against the actual firmware/disassembly, not as a measured fact.
- Assembler/tooling note (13 Sep, bamshanks, self-describing octabam's own existing tooling, not new): the project's `dsp_asm` wrapper (`tools/harness/dsp_host/dsp_asm.cpp`) wraps the vendored `dsp56300/dsp56300` emulator project's assembler (`source/dsp56kEmu/assembler.cpp`) and disassembler (`disasm.cpp`, `disassemble.cpp`) at commit `c051afad31612c2d2c7a81a7ab23e1c5ac9e61af`; the disassembler is separately used against stock payloads via `tools/build/dsp_disasm_all.py`. This matches (does not contradict) the assembler/disassembler trap family already in CLAUDE.md about `dsp_asm` mis-encodings — same underlying vendored tool.
- A different, unrelated DSP56300 tool was mentioned: `mborgerson/dsp56300` — a Rust-based DSP56300 assembler/disassembler/JIT emulator (robottosan_, 13 Sep). bamshanks asked if it had any benefit over the currently-used C++ `dsp56300/dsp56300` tool; no follow-up evaluation happened in this range. Not adopted.

## Policy/process context (not technical, but affects project practice)

- Elektron posted an official statement (16 Sep, linked by j_blanco and .mdee: `elektronauts.com/t/elektronauts-topics-and-comments-regarding-modified-firmware/255891`) that Elektronauts will remove posts/topics about firmware hacking for currently-supported machines and the immediately preceding generation; legacy/unsupported machines were initially carved out, then (17 Sep, amendment at `.../255891/4`) the policy was updated to also restrict development/distribution "for profit or otherwise" that makes "unauthorized use" of their IP, regardless of legacy status.
- This directly reinforces the octamad project's existing stance (already in CLAUDE.md: "Never an Elektron byte in the repo... `.incbin` from the user's stock image at build time is the pattern") — no new technical constraint, but confirms the community-wide pressure behind that rule. A parallel incident (an individual attempting to sell a closed-source Motorola/Monomachine DSP56300 emulator commercially, apparently built on the GPLv3-licensed `dsp56300/dsp56300` codebase per robottosan_) was cited by several members (including bamshanks, "the person trying to sell their md thing is classic ruin it for everyone behaviour") as the likely trigger for Elektron's tightened statement. No octabam-specific action resulted from this in the visible chat.
