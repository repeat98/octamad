[28.08.2026 20:21] ribon3000
threw fable at it - it says it's 16 samples for sure: 

> Settled: OT control frame = 16 samples = 2756.25 Hz, proven from the firmware two ways: (1) the tempo accumulator is an exact identity — frame = 16/44100 s for every BPM (tempo cancels), and (2) a per-track loop clamps trigger offsets to a 16-sample window. The ColdFire control frame and the DSP's 16-sample audio block are the same period, 1:1 — no divider. The "64" floating around is eDMA NBYTES = 64 bytes of DSP status data per frame, not samples. Native LFOs are computed once per frame on the CPU (2756.25 Hz control rate), not per-sample. Internal grid is 96 PPQN with sub-frame-accurate trigs. (Derived from OS 1.40C MKII static analysis; a scope on the DSP→CPU IRQ line should read 2756.25 Hz if anyone wants belt-and-braces.)


extra detail: 

> The sequencer accumulator advances by tempo_raw × 16 per frame (lsll #4 at 0x4000ad46), where tempo is stored as BPM × 24 (clamped [720, 7200] = 30–300 BPM). One MIDI-clock tick costs 2646000 units — and 2646000 = 44100 × 60, i.e. samples per minute. Setting units-accumulated-per-minute equal to units-consumed-per-minute, the tempo cancels entirely and you get frame period = 16/44100 s for every BPM. 
> There's a per-track loop at 0x4000aef2 starting moveq #16,%d6 that computes each track's next-event distance in samples and clamps it into a 0–16-sample window, writing sub-frame trigger offsets. The frame is literally 16 samples wide, and that's how the OT gets sample-accurate timing within the control frame.

```

   ─────────                             ──────────────
   process 16 samples of audio
   using the CURRENT parameter frame
        │
        │  finished a block ──► raises IRQ  ─────►  frame interrupt (0x4000aad0)
        │                                             │
        ▼                                             ├─ swap the parameter double-buffer
   process the NEXT 16 samples  ◄── reads the ────────┤   (front = DSP reads, back = CPU writes)
   from the freshly-filled buffer     new frame       ├─ advance the sequencer phase accumulator

Pretty feasible I think, same approach as our tempo-sync stuff (small caves patched into the ColdFire OS). Stock can't do it because the CC handler only accepts CC 16–45, mapped through a five-page table (PLAYBACK/AMP/LFO/FX1/FX2)  - the recorder setup page is a normal descriptor in the same page table, just not one the CC path can express. [02.09.2026 03:39] bamshanks
Thanks @Bryan T (ukulele owner) 

Nice work again, and thanks. It caught a mislabel on our side too. Went through it against the same image and a few of your open items fell out, here's the short version; the full write-up with the disassembly is in the PR: https://github.com/sambanks/octabam/pull/52

- **The remsl question:** it's a signed divide and the quotient is what's kept. Fed the assembler `divs.l %d2,%d1` and it produced the exact same bytes objdump was labelling `remsl` — the name is just objdump picking one of two mnemonics for the same opcode. So `[0x80001820]` really is the negative reciprocal of the tempo, the reading you'd landed on.

- **Units are samples.** We'd already measured `[0x80001814]` on hardware as BPM×24 (we hang a tempo-sync cave off it). Plug that into your RLEN maths and it comes out as (RLEN+1) × the number of samples in one 16th-note step at 44.1k. So RLEN is steps, lengths are samples, and the 64 floor is 64 samples — four frames.

- **The 992250 table is the FIN/FOUT display ladder itself**, not just fade-shaped: 113 entries, and read as sixteenths of a step it's exactly your `0, 0.063, 0.125 … 64` series. The doubling and the round-and-halve in `0x40005ff0` cancel, so that path is just FOUT converted to samples. One caveat: that only works if the EMAC is in fractional mode, which fits but we haven't read MACSR. Why a pickup arm reads the FOUT slot is still a mystery.

- **Part vs pattern:** you're right that `0x80000003` and `0x100b14cf` are the current part. The writer at `0x40062120` reads it out of the current pattern's record (`[0x80000004]` is the pattern), and the sizes line up neatly: 16 patterns × 0x8ed8 = 0x8ed80, which is exactly where the 0x18b2-stride part records start. We had both labelled "pattern" — fixed.

- **Bit meanings you left open:** from the trig→voice side we have 0x80 = start and 0x10 / 0x8010 / 0xf010 = one-shot / hold / stop / retrig. Our labels, not verified, but same bits.

- The `0x460d17ce` queue's consumer also does RELOAD BANK (opcodes 0x14 and 6), so that's two more of the 46 accounted for.

Still open on both sides: the actual audio write path and where the recorder buffers live.

```
Recorder doc, sessions 2–4: re-verified and three things back

Everything load-bearing in §14 disassembles byte-identical here: pool init, the (track+2)×14602 rows, the segment builder, the pack loop, clrl %fp@(24) at the loop point, the 16-sample extension, the rounding converter, and your one-andil #-16 negative. Three things from our side:

1. §13.2's DMA channel 0 ISR (0x40004860) is not control-surface polling. It's the ColdFire→DSP audio frame transfer: 7-step state machine at 0x46104d3e, TCD 0xFC045000, DSP host port 0x2000001c, 336-word per-track records per ping. We've been feeding effect parameters through it for months, so this one is measured, not inferred. Your "no recorder-table reference" observation is correct and expected — the mixed frame crosses that port, not a buffer.
2. The UI tempo mapping is tempo24 = 24·bpm + (23·tenths + 4)/9, truncating (0x4009c7c4). Tenths 0–9 become 24ths 0 3 5 8 10 13 15 18 20 23, so a displayed .5 is +0.5417 BPM and round(BPM×24) is wrong for seven of ten tenths. Your two fractional rows are both .2, where the rules agree, so your table stands. Under this mapping your 5,279 / 2,017 counts reproduce exactly. (I first claimed they didn't; that was my rule being wrong, retracted the same day.)
3. The 16-sample limit extension is arm-time, not per wrap. 0x40006e2a sits in the arm-calling converter between the length computation and its jsr arm at 0x40006edc, on the same per-track record; the segment builder's wrap path is inline arithmetic and never reaches it. A LOOP=ON buffer armed once sees it once. That closes §14.7 and removes the last way 16 could quantise a loop.

On your 16-sample confusion: I think you're looking at two different 16s. Loop length is not frame-quantised — your own clean rows prove it (88,200, 22,050 and 4,410 are ≡ 8, 2, 10 mod 16). Where 16 plausibly still bites is the relative frame phase of the recorder's read-modify-write and the flex read over the same buffer: there's a 15-sample band of head offsets where every frame returns a new/old mixture, a discontinuity at 2,756 Hz. A sub-sample per-pass rounding error walks the offset into that band. A pass-id simulation (tools/recorder_framephase.py) over your table predicts: zero-error rows clean under either frame order; non-divisible rows seam every frame from pass 2 for about 16/ε passes, then clean; and a strict direction rule. Three of your four clicking rows fit one order, 286.2/2 fits the other, so a single fixed order predicts it clean and your log says it clicks. That's the row to repeat first, plus 198/16 (rounded up, ε = −0.455). Two other cheap discriminators: does the clicking stop after ~16/ε passes; and does a recorded-then-stopped buffer played looped still click. And one question your doc doesn't answer: what exactly is trigged per pass in your patch, a REC trig, a PLAY trig, both, or neither? The mechanism differs for each.

What we're building

We now have your firmware booting under Unicorn (CFV4E core) and, as of today, mounting an emulated CF card and loading a project through the firmware's own storage stack — LOAD PROJECT runs as engine opcode 4 inside the real engine task (your 46-opcode dispatcher), reads project, markers, arrangements and all 16 banks, and the part in RAM matches bank01.work on disk. The aim is to arm a recorder from a loaded part locally and run the write path you traced (0x400068e4 under the per-frame dispatcher 0x4000d2a0) with synthetic input, so the frame-order question above can be settled by watching it rather than guessing. No flash, ~40 s per run.

Things from that work you may want:

- Unicorn's CFV4E core lacks bitrev, byterev, ff1 (ColdFire ISA_C) and raises illegal-instruction on them; 437 sites in the image, 219 byterev, dense in the FAT code. objdump can't decode them either — the .short 0x02c0 you hit in §14 is byterev %d0, 0x00c1/0x04c1 are bitrev/ff1 %d1. We shim them.
- The RTOS boundary for storage was one hook: the PIO handlers only program the task-file registers; the data phase is the ATA ISR at 0x40015304, and the queue primitive 0x4001568c blocks on event wait 0x40000818. Intercepting that wait and doing the transfer there is enough to run the whole FS cold.
- Engine queue creator is 0x40040b14 (queues 0x460d17ce/ee/ae, 1024-entry rings). RELOAD BANK is opcode 20, argument a bitmask of banks, not an index. LOAD PROJECT is posted by 0x40023c7c(name).
- The set-name global (0x100f8480) is an absolute path; the firmware's default is "/PRESETS", and the LOG file it writes to the card root is the best oracle for what a load did.
