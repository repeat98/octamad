# The 909 on the DSP (development, 28 September 2026)

A second 909 engine, written for the DSP cores, replaces the ColdFire 909's
synthesis model. It is wired into both DSP payloads at the source stage.
Both the 808 and 909 now run on the DSP. The retired ColdFire synthesis and its numerical gates have been removed;
ColdFire supplies source controls and UI only. See [DSP808.md](DSP808.md).

## Why a new model

The ColdFire 909 matched Drumazon's init body (correlation 0.9964 from 6 ms)
but not what surrounds it. Measured against 76 Drumazon 2 captures (the
single-knob sweeps, 20 random joint settings, eight velocities, retriggers):

| | v2 (this engine) | ColdFire 909 |
|---|---:|---:|
| waveform error, median / worst | −23.6 / +4.6 dB | −7.0 / +4.2 dB |
| first 12 ms, median | −22.2 dB | −7.7 dB |
| log-spectral distance (3-resolution STFT, 0–500 ms), median / worst | 1.43 / 9.26 dB | 6.79 / 12.37 dB |
| envelope error, median | 0.75 dB | 5.64 dB |

v2 figures are the DSP engine's own output (`eval_dsp.py`, below), desk
neutral. The worst case in every column is ACCNT 1, where both are at about
−80 dB (the desk's 24-bit scaling adds ~1 dB there; no other capture moved). Waveform error
overstates a pitch-sweep difference: a 2 % change of the sweep depth moves
the phase by a fifth of a cycle on a long sweep and reads −5 dB. Joint
settings are judged by the spectral and envelope measures.

What the captures showed, each a stage of the service notes' BD circuit:

- **Pitch**: `f(t) = fb + A·exp(−t/τ)` from the trig, exact to 0.001 cycles
  over 56 cycles (a linear VCO under the C9 envelope). PITCH moves fb and A,
  TUNE moves τ (3.4–32 ms), TUNE DEPTH scales A as `(2u)^2.3`.
- **Release**: the triangle is held at reset and released rising from zero
  2.476 ms after the trig (sample 109.2), not held at its crest to 3 ms.
- **Click**: the trigger pulse ends when the released triangle first
  reaches 0.505 of its peak (the comparator path), so the click moves with
  pitch: 2.95 ms at init, 4.9 ms at TUNE DEPTH 0, 6.3 ms at the lowest pitch.
  Its edge rings at 5.83 kHz, Q 1.86. ATTACK scales it exactly linearly;
  6 % remains at ATTACK 0.
- **Shaper**: static and asymmetric (H2 −26 dB, H3 −30 dB), before a
  linear VCA: the harmonics do not change over a 45 dB decay, and the
  harmonic profile is the same at 30, 53 and 100 Hz (a curve, not a
  filter). The steady cycle is time-symmetric to −30…−41 dB, so the curve is
  read off it directly over the triangle. The first model's degree-7 fit ran
  H13–H64 8–13 dB above Drumazon and H6–H8 10 dB below: the user heard "too
  many overtones in the body" (28 Sep 2026). Degree 11 is within 2 dB in
  every band to H32 (H1 and DC held to the time-domain fit).
- **VCA**: flat for 60 ms (−0.3 dB droop) at every DECAY, then the DECAY
  release (τ 8.7 ms … 453 ms).
- **Thump**: the output is DC-coupled. A slow negative offset follows the
  envelope through a 0.405 s low-pass (the VCA's control feedthrough); its
  size saturates with the sweep depth. Sub-audio, but it sets headroom.
- **Noise**: free-running (it differs by note position), HP 230 Hz and two
  poles at 2.37 kHz, gated by an attack/decay envelope (5.1 / 4.3 ms).
- **Velocity**: body level (v/100)², attack by table.
- **Retrigger**: monophonic hard reset of VCO and VCA; the thump and filters
  carry over.

These are measurements of Drumazon, not of a TR-909. Drumazon agreement does
not establish agreement with a physical unit.

## The engine

`bd909.asm`, 524 words, one voice per call, 16-sample blocks. Knobs are
decoded once per block from 128-entry tables, so p-locks and LFOs apply
within a note. Per sample: pitch envelope and VCO, triangle, degree-11
Horner shaper (at S/16: its partial sums reach 2.8 at S/4), VCA and thump
(48-bit states, so long decays neither stall nor leave DC), pulse (HP +
biquad + LP), LCG noise (HP + 2 LP + ENV2), the optional 909 output
network, one clip point, then the desk. Output scale 0.4: Drumazon's init
peak lands at 0.39, a full-ATTACK click at 0.92 (the desk's ceiling
softens it from 0.996).

### The desk: SAT, LOW, HIGH

A Mackie-desk stage after the voice, asked for by the user with airwindows
MackEQ (Chris Johnson, MIT) as the reference, not to be ported 1:1. Kept
from MackEQ: the curve (a clip at 1, then `x − 0.1768·x⁵`) at the input, in
the bass and high bands and at the output stage; the band drive law `4u²`;
the one-pole crossover coefficients (0.159, 0.236 at 44.1 kHz); the output
stage 9 dB above the input stage; the AC-coupled input (its 13 Hz pole).
Changed: the split is complementary and each band's output is `√(2u)`, so
LOW 64 / HIGH 64 is flat (MackEQ's defaults are +8.8 dB with a 4.7 dB dip
at 2 kHz), and the input coupling ramps in over SAT 0–16 so SAT 0 is the
DC-coupled, Drumazon-calibrated 909 (−75 dB from dry on the init patch).
Dropped: MackEQ's darkening pole after the bass band's curve (≤1.8 dB, only
at extreme settings; the output stage dominates) and its four 19.16 kHz
biquads.

- **SAT** (SRC F): 0–36 dB of drive. The make-up holds the init patch's
  level rise to +6 dB at SAT 127 (measured +4.7 dB RMS; the peaks fall from
  0.39 to 0.17 as the desk compresses). The curve tops out at 0.823, so the
  make-up is capped at 1.215 and the output never hard-clips.
- **LOW / HIGH** (SETUP D, E): each band's gain, 64 flat, 127 +15 dB (and
  4× the drive into that band's curve), 32 −15 dB, 0 off. A stored 0 is a
  full cut: new assignments default to 64.

The coupling state is 48-bit: at 24 bits its 0.00186 coefficient stalls
short of the input, and at full SAT that residue became a +0.0038 DC floor
under every tail (found by the gate, −20.7 dB → −84.5 dB).

Every multiply uses a signed operand order, no accumulator-to-accumulator
ALU form is used, and every instruction form has a site in the stock
payloads. The 808 uses stock `asl #$10,a,a` and `do a` forms;
the desk harness uses stock `do n7`.
`dsp909.assemble` decodes its own bytes with `dsp56kDisassemble` and
refuses any address whose mnemonic or operands differ from the source, or
any su/uu/max/rnd form. The first version grepped the listing, which echoes
the source text: `mac -y0,y1,b` went out as `macsu` and only the gate
caught it (AGENTS.md).

| DSP vs its float reference (`dsp909.Voice`), eleven voice cases | −69.9 … −100.9 dB |
| the desk alone, same 24-bit input both sides, four cases | −68.0 … −91.8 dB |

[CPU.md](CPU.md). The optimized voice costs 322.125 executed instructions/sample

cycles; hardware headroom requires measurement on the unit.

## Reproduce

```sh
python3 tools/harness/bd909.py [--wav]            # build, run all gate cases, meter
                                                  # (bd909_host -input feeds the desk alone)
python3 tools/harness/benchmark_stock_dsp.py      # the stock baseline
```

The fitting and comparison scripts that produced `fit909.json` and the
Drumazon tables above live in `out/analog-bassdrum/` of this worktree
(`fit_body.py`, `fit_pulse2.py`, `fit_noise.py`, `fit_pitch2.py`,
`fit_decay.py`, `fit_tune0.py`, `evaluate.py`, `evaluate2.py`,
`eval_dsp.py`); they need the captures and the scipy environment there and
are **not yet in the tree**. `drumazon_host` now also takes
`DRUMAZON_VEL`, `DRUMAZON_NOTES` and `DRUMAZON_SECONDS`.

## Image integration

The donor region also contains a 35-word routine called by retained DARK reverb.
The builder preserves it at the end of the region, relocates its DO endpoint,
and retargets the three external calls in each payload. A stock/patched audio
identity gate protects both PLATE and DARK on both cores.

`tools/build/ab_image.py` places the glue and voice in SPRING REV's private
P region (602 words, A `0x1252`, B `0x1012`), nulls its two dispatch entries, and
patches the source seam (A `0x39c`, B `0x1a2`). SPRING REV is removed from
the remix. The guard refuses DSP-module compositions and MACHINEDRUM;
this placement is currently qualified only with stock track effects.

Tables occupy private X `0x2840..0x30ff`; four voice blocks per core occupy
`0x3100..0x31ff`, 64 words each (48 state, 12 knobs, one trigger offset).
Both complete uploads are delivered by the platform's pre-boot loader.
The raw and packed uploads each have a 256 KiB staging limit. No shared
window memory is taken from the stock effects.

`ab_render` sends a control record at the track's fixed record boundary,
including on the first hit, when the preceding renderer can still be FLEX.
Its first two longs contain the `ab09` / `0909` signature and trigger flag;
the next two are reserved, then six longs carry twelve knob halfwords.
HDI transport adds tags above bit 15: the glue masks these before comparing
signatures or using knob indices. The trigger offset comes from stock
X:`0x20c`. The voice writes sixteen stereo frames at X:0, advances the
stock per-track FLEX ring base, then continues at A `0x426` / B `0x221`:
stock AMP, FX1, FX2 and the packer remain downstream.

Measured here: standalone DSP and desk reference gates pass (worst −68 dB);
the ColdFire control-record gate covers all sixteen offsets and the next
record's boundary; the full port produces 808 on T1 and DSP 909 on T5,
post-FX and main output, with no staged sample. The swapped assignment
also passes (DSP 909 T1, now DSP 808 T5); payload B's full-image source
agrees with `dsp909.Voice` at −83.4 dB. The panel gate verifies
MODEL, LPF, SAT/LOW/HIGH descriptors and neutral band defaults. Build
refhash: all 24 existing configurations and reports bit-identical.
`OT_PROJECT=out/analog-bassdrum/ui-fixture make check REMIX=analog-bassdrum`
passed after integration, including the module's port and panel gates.

The earlier claim that pristine stock boot requires an opcode-cache bounds
check is retracted. On this rebased macOS build, the unguarded port boots
stock with DSP enabled and completes a project load and panel script. No
out-of-range invalidation was observed. The unrelated port change has
been removed from this PR; no reproducible failing configuration is known.

The user auditioned the integrated image in Octemu on 28 September 2026
and approved the 909 voicing. Its launched copy is kept under
`out/analog-bassdrum/octemu-dsp909/analog-bassdrum.os`; this is listening
feedback, separate from the hardware and numerical qualifications.
